THE CENTRAL QUESTION

What makes a good
world-action representation?

World-action models need a shared space that supports both predicting future observations and choosing robot actions. Visual reconstruction quality alone does not tell us how useful that space is for control.

ReWAM starts from frozen DINO features, calibrates them for dynamics learning, and organizes them into temporal world states. The action objective shapes these states; the world objective learns their evolution. This division of roles supports strong robot policies without generative video pre-training.

THE METHOD

Perception, organized for action.

Three components turn perceptual features into a space for prediction and control.

01 / CALIBRATE
F

Feature Calibration

Multi-layer aggregation, normalization, and a representation-aware noise schedule prepare frozen DINO features for diffusion-based dynamics modeling.

02 / COMPRESS
t − 1t→z

Temporal Representation Bottleneck

A trainable bottleneck combines paired frames across camera views into compact, 128-dimensional world-state tokens.

03 / GROUND

Action-Grounded Representation Shaping

Only action-loss gradients update the bottleneck. World-loss gradients stop at the representations, allowing the world model to learn their dynamics.
